About

Shepherd is an orbital digital twin developed as part of a student entrepreneurship project at ESTACA. The site tracks catalogued objects in real time from their orbital elements (TLE), and serves as a basis for studying laser nudging manoeuvres to prevent collisions in low Earth orbit.

Data sources

Orbital elements (TLE) and satellite catalogue (country, object type): CelesTrak, fetched on demand and cached locally. Orbit propagation: SGP4/SDP4 via satellite.js for TLE-based objects; numerical integration (Dormand-Prince 5(4), J2-J6 EGM-96, drag, Moon, Sun, radiation pressure) for synthetic objects. Conjunctions: closest approach point and probability of collision (Foster method, covariance estimated from the TLE age). Globe and 3D rendering: CesiumJS.

Satellite 3D model: “Starlink Spacex Satellite” by Malacodart, licensed under CC BY 4.0 (textures reduced to 1024 px).

3D models of the ISS, Hubble and generic satellites (ICESat, Landsat 4/5, MiRaTa, RADARSAT-1, SAC-C, SSL-1300, GOES): NASA 3D Resources (public domain). Shepherd is neither affiliated with nor endorsed by NASA.

Model limitations

Displayed positions are computed from public TLEs, whose accuracy degrades as they age. Collision probabilities rely on a covariance estimated from the TLE age (public TLEs do not provide one): they rank threats and are not certified. Demonstration and study tool — not qualified for real operational decision-making.
